# DESIGN.md

The visual system of **Universe Library**: colour, type, scale, shape, depth, motion, imagery, the logo, and the rules that keep them consistent. Every visual decision belongs here. Engineering decisions belong in `AGENTS.md`; where the two meet (the 3D scene's brightness, for instance), this file gives the intent and `AGENTS.md` gives the numbers.

- **Tokens:** [static/Styles/Tokens.css](static/Styles/Tokens.css). It is the only place a colour, size, radius or duration is defined, and every stylesheet loads it first. Prefix `--ul-`.
- **Generators:** [design/palette.py](design/palette.py) (the colour ramps) and [design/logo.py](design/logo.py) (the logo, favicon and loader art). Change the generator and regenerate; never hand-edit a generated shade or path.
- **Method:** the proportions come from [LiftKit](https://github.com/Chainlift/liftkit), described in §4. We borrow its formulas, not its code: LiftKit's own README marks the library "not recommended for production use", and it targets Next.js.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **A planetarium, not a textbook.** The sky is the canvas and fills the screen. Interface is glass laid over it, never a page with a picture in it.
2. **Colour is light, and light means something.** Each brand hue is a kind of light the galaxy actually emits: amber for old stars, blue for young ones, rose for hydrogen, violet for imagination. Use a colour for what it means, not because a spot needs brightening.
3. **Quiet chrome, loud content.** Panels are dark, translucent and low-contrast at rest. The galaxy, the labels on it and the photographs carry the colour.
4. **One ratio.** Every size, step and type size derives from the golden ratio (1.618) and its fractional steps. When a value is needed, pick a step; don't invent a number.
5. **Optical, not arithmetic.** Centre things the way they look centred: trim padding beside the extra space a line-height adds, and nest radii concentrically (§4, §6).
6. **Honest at a glance.** Visual conventions carry truth. Fiction is always dashed, violet and italic. "Shown closer" and "log-scaled" notes are never hidden for looks. A painted object never looks like a measured one.
7. **Additive by nature.** The scene is additive light, so black is "nothing", not a colour. Nothing in the scene can paint dark; darkness comes from light not being emitted (dust, extinction). Photographs sit on black for the same reason.

## 2. Brand

### 2.1 Name and voice
- **Universe Library**, set as an uppercase, widely tracked eyebrow (`--ul-tracking-eyebrow`, `.3em`) in Sunglow Amber above the page title.
- Titles name the place ("Milky Way"); leads are one plain sentence ("A map of our galaxy. Choose a place to visit, or wander.").
- Labels on the map are uppercase with light tracking; subtitles are uppercase and dim.

### 2.2 The logo
A face-on, two-armed spiral galaxy. Every part maps to the palette and to what the site is:

| Part | Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Core | white → Sunglow Amber `#ffd9a0` | the old stars of the bulge |
| Arms, inner → outer | Amber → Starlight Blue `#9ecbff` → Nebula Violet `#c7a6ff` → violet-600 `#8f69c7` | stars get younger outwards; violet is the house tint |

The arms are log spirals at about a 22° pitch, trailing: they wind anticlockwise outwards on screen, so the galaxy turns clockwise, and that is how the loader spins it.

**Rules**
- Clear space: a quarter of the mark's width on every side. Never place it on a busy part of the galaxy without a glass panel behind it.
- Minimum size: 24 px for the full mark. Below that, use the bold favicon mark: at 16 px the faint arms, knots and Sun turn to noise.
- The mark is always on dark: Space 1000, the sky or glass. For a light background, use the favicon badge, which brings its own dark.
- Don't recolour, outline, rotate to a fixed angle, add a drop shadow, or stretch it. A soft blue glow (`drop-shadow(0 0 10px rgba(158,203,255,.22))`) is the only allowed effect.
- Spin it only as a loading indicator: clockwise, 9 s per turn, and 60 s under reduced motion.

### 3.4 Contrast and which steps to use
Measured with WCAG 2 contrast against the glass panel, which comes out at about `#10141f`:

| Steps | Ratio on glass | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 50–300 | 10.8–17.1 | text of any size; key colours |
| 400–500 | 6.1–9.0 | text of any size; secondary text in colour |
| 600 | 4.3–4.7 | **large text (≥ 18.7 px bold or 24 px) and icons only** |
| 700–950 | below 3 | fills, containers, glows, outlines, dark gradients; never text |

- Ink is 15.3:1 and ink-dim 7.2:1. Ink-faint is **3.9:1, which fails AA for small text**: use it only for hints, disabled states and decoration, never for information.
- On a colour fill (a pressed chip, a badge), put `--ul-on-*` (Space 1000) on top, never white.

### 3.5 Gradients and glow
- **The brand gradient** runs amber → blue → violet: old to young to imagined. Use it for the logo arms, the loader bar (`#9ecbff → #ffd9a0` today) and nothing that is only decorative.
- **Glow** is the colour itself at 0 offset with a negative spread (`--ul-glow-sm`/`--ul-glow-md` plus a colour). A glow means "lit" or "selected". Keep it to one glowing element per group.
- Never put text in a gradient on the galaxy page. The system pages' gradient titles are legacy and are not to be copied.

### 3.7 Don'ts
- No pure, fully saturated sRGB primaries (`#f00`, `#00f`): they don't exist in the sky and they shout.
- No red-for-error convention. If an error ever needs a colour, it is rose-400 with words that say what went wrong.
- No new hue without a ramp from `palette.py` and a row in §3.1 or §3.6.
- No white panels. A light surface would read as a hole cut in the sky.

## 4. Scale and spacing (LiftKit)

### 4.1 The ratio
LiftKit's scale factor is the golden ratio, plus three fractional steps for fine adjustment. Values from LiftKit's `liftkitvars.css`:

| Token | Value | Is |
|---|---|---|
| `--ul-wholestep` | 1.618 | φ |
| `--ul-halfstep` | 1.272 | √φ |
| `--ul-quarterstep` | 1.128 | φ^¼ |
| `--ul-eighthstep` | 1.062 | φ^⅛ |

### 4.2 Sizes
`md` is 1 rem (16 px), and each named step is one φ away. This is LiftKit's 3xs–4xl, in rem rather than em so that nested font sizes don't compound:

| Token | rem | px |
|---|---|---|
| `--ul-size-3xs` | .146 | 2.3 |
| `--ul-size-2xs` | .236 | 3.8 |
| `--ul-size-xs` | .382 | 6.1 |
| `--ul-size-sm` | .618 | 9.9 |
| `--ul-size-md` | 1 | 16 |
| `--ul-size-lg` | 1.618 | 25.9 |
| `--ul-size-xl` | 2.618 | 41.9 |
| `--ul-size-2xl` | 4.236 | 67.8 |
| `--ul-size-3xl` | 6.854 | 109.7 |
| `--ul-size-4xl` | 11.09 | 177.4 |

- Gaps between panels and the screen edge: `md` (`--galaxy-gap`, 10 px on phones).
- Padding inside panels: `md` horizontally and `sm`–`md` vertically. Between rows: `2xs`–`xs`. Between an icon and its text: `xs`–`sm`.
- Two sizes next to each other should be at least one full step apart, so the difference reads as intended.

### 4.3 Optical symmetry
- **Text containers:** a line box adds space above the cap height, so equal padding looks bottom-heavy. LiftKit trims the side next to text by an offset of `font-size × line-height ÷ 1.618`: for body text at a 1.618 line-height that is exactly 1 em less. In practice, give a text-first card a little less top padding than bottom (our panels use 14 px top against 10–18 px elsewhere, which is this idea by eye).
- **Icon + text buttons:** an icon's box is emptier than a word's. Pad the icon side by one step less than the text side.
- **Round things inside square things** (a dot in a row, the logo in a header) sit on the text's cap height, not on its box centre.

### 5.3 Setting
- **Tracking:** titles `-0.022em` (LiftKit), map labels `.1–.12em` (`--ul-tracking-label`), eyebrows and caplines `.28–.3em` (`--ul-tracking-eyebrow`). Uppercase always gets positive tracking. Mixed case never does.
- **Case:** uppercase for eyebrows, map labels, subtitles and readouts. Sentence case for titles, leads, rows and notes. Never use title case.
- **Figures:** use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` wherever numbers update or line up (readouts, catalogue columns).
- **Measure:** running text stays under about 65 characters per line.
- **Never below 0.6 rem** (9.6 px), and only for uppercase captions.
- Labels on the sky get a black text-shadow halo (`0 0 4px #000, 0 0 10px #000`) instead of a box, so the sky stays visible.

## 6. Shape

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ul-radius-xs` | 2 px | diamonds, ticks |
| `--ul-radius-sm` | 11 px | rows, chips, small cards, map labels |
| `--ul-radius-md` | 18 px | panels |
| `--ul-radius-lg` | 22 px | dialogs, the logo badge (22% of its size) |
| `--ul-radius-pill` | 999 px | buttons, readouts, the layer row, toggles |
| — | 50% | dots, rings, round buttons |

- **Nested radii are concentric:** inner radius = outer radius − the padding between them. A chip with `sm` (11 px) inside a panel with `md` (18 px) wants about 7 px of padding to line up; with more padding, the inner corner gets sharper, never rounder than the outer.
- **The marker language is shape, not colour:** a ring is a star system, a hollow ring a system without a page, a diamond any other object, and a dashed edge fiction. Strokes are 1.5 px. Keep this language in every new marker, list dot and legend swatch.
- Icons are drawn on a 24-unit grid with round caps and joins, 1.5–2 unit strokes, and no fills except dots (see the layer buttons in `Universe.html`).

## 7. Depth: glass and glow

There are no drop shadows on black. Depth is made of translucency, blur, hairlines and light.

| Layer | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Sky | the 3D scene; `#000` |
| Map labels | no box, with a black text halo, links included; a link with a page has a bright, bold name. Always the small size |
| Map dots | a 9 px box holding a 5 px glyph in the place's colour, a 1 px dark halo and a 5 px glow; the shape says the kind (dot, ringed dot, hollow, diamond, soft glow, specks, thin ring). Never bigger, never a big glow: many more places are coming |
| Controls, readouts | `--ul-glass` + `--ul-blur-md` + an `--ul-outline` hairline, pill |
| Every panel: the dock's panels, the layer row, settings (button and dialog), the legend, the layer tooltip, the open place | **one surface** (owner, 2026-11-26): `--ul-panel-fill` (near-opaque, `rgba(24,28,50,.9)` to `rgba(8,10,20,.94)`) + the travelling edge as its only border, **no backdrop blur** (it showed a sliver of blurred sky past the right and bottom edges of panels with fractional sizes, and cost a blur of the canvas per panel per frame); `md` radius, `lg` for dialogs and the open place. The legend adds a violet, amber and blue gradient drifting behind its keys (28 s) |
| Loader | a radial vignette, `#0b1020` → `#000` |

- **Selection and hover** brighten: background to `--ul-state-hover`/`--ul-state-pressed`, hairline to the role colour, plus a `--ul-glow-md` in the role colour. They never darken.
- **The travelling edge** (2026-11-26): every panel's border is a 1.5 px (`--ul-edge-width`) conic gradient, amber into violet with a cool blue between (`--ul-edge-stops`), turning once every 14 s (`--ul-edge-period`, `@property --ul-edge-angle`), over a soft glow in amber and violet (`--ul-edge-glow`). It is the one moving thing in the chrome; it stops under reduced motion. Draw it with a masked `::after` at `inset: 0` on a panel with **no border of its own** (see `GalaxyStyles.css`), so the ring and the panel share one outline: at 1 px over a separate 1 px border the ring sat inside the border box with the outer radius, and its corners stepped (owner, 2026-11-26). A panel that scrolls must scroll an inner element, not itself.
- **Scrollbars** (2026-11-26): never the native grey. A slim rounded thumb (8 px track, 2 px inset) lit amber into violet into blue at half strength, full strength under the pointer, on no track (`::-webkit-scrollbar-*` in `GalaxyStyles.css`), in the panel's right margin rather than over the rows: the scroller reaches over the padding by the bar's width, with `scrollbar-gutter: stable`. Chromium ignores those pseudo-elements once `scrollbar-color` is set, so set `scrollbar-color` only for browsers without them (`@supports not selector(::-webkit-scrollbar)`), where it is a thin violet bar. A panel that is pushed or squeezed (the legend under a tooltip) clips and fades rather than growing a scrollbar.
- LiftKit's shadow set (`shadow-sm` to `2xl`) is for light surfaces. We don't use it; if a light surface is ever added, take its values from there.

## 8. Motion

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ul-duration-fast` | .2 s | hover, press |
| `--ul-duration-base` | .35 s | panel open and fold, layout shifts, the readout sliding |
| `--ul-duration-slow` | .6 s | whole layers fading, the loader lifting |
| — | 1.6 s | camera flights (`flyTo`), cubic in-out, log-interpolated distance |
| `--ul-ease` / `--ul-ease-out` | `ease` / `cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)` | general / things arriving |

- **Nothing moves on its own.** No auto-rotation, no idle drift, no decorative loops except the loader.
- Labels fade; they never pop. Layers cross-fade by distance, never switch at a threshold.
- **Reduced motion:** flights become cuts, damping turns off, CSS transitions are removed, and the loader slows to 60 s per turn.

## 9. Imagery

- **Photographs are light on black.** They are composited additively and premultiplied onto black, so their dark sky disappears into ours. Never use an image with a white or transparent-checkerboard background on the map.
- Sources: Wikimedia Commons only, public domain or CC0/CC BY/CC BY-SA, downloaded and never hotlinked, each with its credit shown on the card and recorded in `starsystems/image_sources.py`.
- Galaxy photographs are median-filtered so their foreground stars don't compete with ours. Card photos are 960 px wide.
- An open place is **drawn where it is** - a star by its temperature, a pulsar, a black hole, a cloud, a cluster, a small cube for things we built - and the bordered glass panel on the right points at it with a **leader**: a 1.2 px line in the place's colour with a soft glow, from the panel's edge at the name, to an elbow, to a small ring with four ticks on the object. The line draws itself in over 0.8 s. Stars and machines are drawn larger than life and say so; clouds and clusters are at their real size. It appears only for the place you clicked, and the view is locked while it is up. No photographs.
- **Fiction may use images from games, films and books** (the owner's choice), always with the reference shown under them. They show like any photo, with a dashed violet edge, the fiction convention, and "Reference image:" in the credit.
- An artist's impression is labelled as one (the backdrop, the black holes' glyphs).
- The technical recipe (ffmpeg settings, sizes, rate limits) is in `AGENTS.md` §7.8.

## 10. Accessibility

- Contrast as in §3.4: AA for all information. Ink-faint is never the only carrier of meaning.
- **Focus is always visible:** `--ul-focus-ring` on controls; the map canvas gets an inset version.
- Hit targets are at least 40 × 40 px (the zoom buttons are 48 × 42); on touch, 44 px.
- Never use colour alone: fiction is dashed **and** italic **and** violet, and every marker type differs in shape.
- Every icon button has an `aria-label`. No hover pop-ups, with one exception: a layer switch shows its tooltip under the row - name, what it shows, its part of the legend, its state - and the legend slides down rather than being covered.
- The legend is one line per key: swatch, name in ink, a few words in dim ink at .94 em. Keep the words short enough to fit 268 px.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (§8).

## 11. The 3D scene's look (intent)

- The galaxy is luminous but never blown out: an HDR target, a shouldered tone curve and a modest bloom. Highlights roll off; they don't clip to white shapes.
- The disk leans violet and pink; the core stays gold; **dust is black** (the owner's choice, 2026-11-24): lanes absorb almost evenly across colours (`DUST_COLOR`), so they read as darkness rather than rust. That is the same amber–blue–violet story as the brand gradient, told by the scene.
- Brightness is a hierarchy: the Milky Way first, then the named neighbours by distance, then the painted landmarks, then the anonymous far field. Something lower in that list must never outshine something above it.
- Numbers, measurements and the rules for changing them are in `AGENTS.md` §7.

2. **Font sizes**: there are 25 distinct sizes, from .6 to 2 rem. Map each to the nearest `--ul-ui-*` step: .62–.64 → caption, .66–.68 → label, .74–.78 → callout, .8–.84 → body, .95 → heading, 1–1.2 → title3, 1.35–1.5 → title2.
3. **JS colours**: the scene and legend colours in `config.js`, `landmarks.js` and `view.js` duplicate some token values. Read them from CSS (`getComputedStyle`) or a shared `palette.js` so §3.6 has one source.
4. **System pages**: deferred by the owner. When they are rebuilt, they load `Tokens.css`, use `--ul-font-ui`, and drop gradient text and inline styles.

## 13. Checklist for any visual change

- [ ] Used a role or token, not a literal; or added the token first.
- [ ] Colour means what §3 says it means; no new hue without a ramp.
- [ ] Text contrast follows §3.4.
- [ ] Sizes are scale steps; adjacent sizes are a full step apart.
- [ ] Radii are concentric; markers follow the shape language.
- [ ] Depth comes from glass and glow, not shadows.
- [ ] Motion uses the duration tokens and respects reduced motion.
- [ ] Checked on a dark sky at desktop size and at 375 × 812, with labels on and off.
